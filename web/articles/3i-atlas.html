3I/ATLAS: a visitor from another star

The third confirmed interstellar object crossed our Solar System in 2025. See how repeated infrared observations can help tell its story.

BLINK Field Notes·First reported July 1, 2025
☄

A comet with an origin beyond the Sun

Most comets we watch are bound to the Sun. 3I/ATLAS follows a different kind of path: its trajectory shows it is passing through the Solar System from interstellar space. The “3I” designation marks it as the third confirmed interstellar object observed here.

The NASA-funded ATLAS survey first reported observations on July 1, 2025. Follow-up observations let astronomers track the comet and study its activity as sunlight warmed its icy material.

Why catch it in infrared?

Infrared light can reveal thermal emission and spectral features from dust and gas around a comet. Repeated observations also show how its apparent position changes against the background sky. The two clues answer different questions: what the comet is doing, and where it is going.
